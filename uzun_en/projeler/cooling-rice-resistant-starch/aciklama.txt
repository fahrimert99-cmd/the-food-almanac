Cooled Rice and Blood Sugar: What Studies Show

Can cooling cooked rice, potatoes or pasta soften the blood sugar spike? In small crossover trials in healthy adults, rice that was cooled in the refrigerator and then reheated produced a modestly smaller glucose response than freshly cooked rice, about 18 percent in one trial of 15 people. The effect was smaller for pasta and unclear for potatoes.

In this video we look at what happens to starch when it cools, what the trials actually measured, why parboiled rice made a bigger difference in one study, what has not been tested in older adults or in people with diabetes, and how to cool and reheat rice safely so that Bacillus cereus does not become a problem.
