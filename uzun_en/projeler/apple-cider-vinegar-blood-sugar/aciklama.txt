Does Apple Cider Vinegar Lower Blood Sugar?

In a small trial of 12 healthy young adults, a drink of liquid vinegar before a starchy meal made the blood sugar rise in the first hour about 31 percent smaller than water did. Pooled reviews agree that vinegar can modestly lower the after-meal rise, but the trials are small and short, and reviewers rate the certainty of the evidence as very low.

In this video we look at what the studies actually found, how acetic acid may slow the stomach, why the evidence is thinner than the headlines, and the safety points about teeth, the throat, tablets and medication. We finish with practical, careful ways to use vinegar, if you choose to.
