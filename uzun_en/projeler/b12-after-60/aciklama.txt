Why Is B12 Deficiency So Common After 60?

Low vitamin B12 is easy to miss and surprisingly common in older adults. In a study of 3,299 adults aged 60 and older, 38 percent of those with atrophic gastritis, a thinning of the stomach lining, were low in B12, compared with 15 percent of a comparison group. Two very common kinds of medicine, metformin and acid reducers, have also been linked with lower B12 over time.

In this video we look at why aging stomachs struggle to release B12 from food, what the metformin, proton-pump inhibitor and H2 blocker studies actually found, why B12 in fortified foods and supplements is easier to absorb, the quiet signs that are easy to blame on age, and when it makes sense to ask your doctor for a blood test.
