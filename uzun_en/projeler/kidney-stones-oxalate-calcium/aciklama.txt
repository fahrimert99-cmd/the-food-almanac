Calcium and Kidney Stones: What Studies Show

Many people are told to cut dairy and spinach after a kidney stone. In a five-year trial of 120 men with recurrent calcium oxalate stones, a low-calcium diet did worse than a normal-calcium diet that also limited salt and animal protein: 23 of 60 men formed new stones, against 12 of 60. Large studies of tens of thousands of adults also linked calcium from food with fewer stones, and calcium pills with more.

In this video we look at those studies, why calcium and oxalate can bind in the gut, what the research says about spinach, water, salt, animal protein and soft drinks, and why the right diet depends on the type of stone. We also cover who should check with a doctor first, and six practical steps.
