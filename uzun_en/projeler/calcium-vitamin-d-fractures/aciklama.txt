After 60: Do Calcium and Vitamin D Prevent Fractures?

In VITAL, a trial of nearly 26,000 American adults over 50, five years of vitamin D did not lower the risk of broken bones. In the Women's Health Initiative, calcium plus vitamin D raised hip bone density a little, did not significantly lower fractures, and was linked with more kidney stones. Yet in an older trial of women in care homes, the two together did seem to help.

In this video we look at what those trials and the 2018 US Preventive Services Task Force statement found, why the answer differs for healthy people living at home and for frail people in care, how calcium from food such as yogurt, milk and canned salmon with bones compares with pills, what we know about kidney stones and heart risk, and when doctors do prescribe treatment for osteoporosis and osteopenia.
