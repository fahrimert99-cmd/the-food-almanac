## Summary

All 14 sources exist and say what the script attributes to them; no citation is fabricated or mismatched, and every source in `kaynaklar` is used. The core claim (a small LDL drop of about 4.5 mg/dL, firmer for extract than for the drink) matches Xu 2020 and NCCIH's own wording ("to a small extent"), and the script does not contradict mainstream guidance: it says green tea does not replace statins and carries the liver, caffeine and drug-interaction cautions.

One real error was found. In scene 21 the script and chart said LDL fell 0.9% in the placebo group; the abstract reports a 0.9% **rise**. The narration, chart title ("Change in LDL cholesterol over 12 months") and placebo bar label ("+0.9%") were corrected. Bar `deger` values were left as magnitudes; the labels carry the direction.

Four small wording fixes: FDA's 400 mg figure is for "most adults"; the warfarin patient's INR "rose again" (to 2.55, not fully back to 3.79); EFSA's 5-cup finding is now flagged as limited evidence; and "well-brewed" became "average-strength" because the two-cup figure uses USDA averages.

Opening promises are all paid off: cups (scene 52), medicines (45–49), the safety finding (35), and the brew-strength range (53). Title and thumbnail (−4.5 mg/dL) match the video and Xu 2020.

Two limits on this check: Booth 1995 has no abstract in the record, so only its title supports scene 46; and the full texts of Samavat 2016 and Dostal 2015 were not read, only their abstracts.
