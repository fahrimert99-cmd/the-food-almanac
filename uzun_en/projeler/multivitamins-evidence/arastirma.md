# Research notes: Do daily multivitamins actually do anything after 60?

Slug: `multivitamins-evidence`. Audience: US adults 55+, many on metformin, acid reducers or warfarin.

**Access note.** PubMed, PMC and NIH ODS pages often returned cookie walls or HTTP 403 when fetched directly. Items marked **(page opened)** were read on the page itself; items marked **(abstract via search)** rest on abstract text or summaries returned by search, not the full paper. The script words those claims cautiously. I could not run the validator or word counter in this session (shell blocked); word count was estimated by hand at about 1,600 narration words.

## 1. COSMOS-Mind: multivitamin and cognition in older adults **(abstract via search)**
- **Citation:** Baker LD, Manson JE, Rapp SR, et al. Effects of cocoa extract and a multivitamin on cognitive function: A randomized clinical trial. *Alzheimer's & Dementia.* 2023;19(4):1308-1319. (Index records differ on author order; the script says "Laura Baker and colleagues".)
- **Design/population:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled 2×2 factorial (cocoa extract, 500 mg flavanols/day, and a daily multivitamin-mineral), 3 years, cognitive testing by telephone each year. 2,262 enrolled; mean age 73; 60% women; 89% non-Hispanic White. Ages 60+ (COSMOS eligibility).
- **Figures:** Multivitamin: global cognition mean z = 0.07 (95% CI 0.02 to 0.12; P = .007). Benefits also reported for memory and executive function. Larger in people with a history of cardiovascular disease (0.14, 95% CI −0.02 to 0.31, imprecise). Cocoa extract: global cognition 0.03 (95% CI −0.02 to 0.08; P = .28), no effect. No significant cocoa × multivitamin interaction.
- **Authors' caution:** Baker was quoted in press coverage: "It's too early to recommend daily multivitamin supplementation to prevent cognitive decline." Authors call for confirmation in a more diverse cohort.
- **Limitations:** Mostly non-Hispanic White; test scores, not dementia diagnoses; telephone-based tests; small effect size; subgroup (heart disease) not adjusted for multiple comparisons. Press estimates of "60% slowing / 1.8 years" vary by source and are **not used** in the script.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/ (search "Effects of cocoa extract and a multivitamin on cognitive function"); record: https://nutrition-evidence.com/article/447444/

## 2. COSMOS-Clinic and pooled meta-analysis of 3 COSMOS cognitive studies **(page opened via fetch summary; PMC full text blocked)**
- **Citation:** Vyas CM, Manson JE, Sesso HD, et al. Effect of multivitamin-mineral supplementation versus placebo on cognitive function: results from the clinic subcohort of the COSMOS trial and meta-analysis of 3 cognitive studies within COSMOS. *Am J Clin Nutr.* 2024;119(3):692-701. doi:10.1016/j.ajcnut.2023.12.011. PMID 38244989. PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11103094/
- **Design/population:** COSMOS is a 2×2 factorial trial of cocoa extract and multivitamin-mineral vs placebo in US adults aged 60+ (about 21,400 randomized; sources give 21,422 and 21,442, so the script says "more than 21,000"). Meta-analysis pooled non-overlapping participants: COSMOS-Clinic n = 573, COSMOS-Mind n = 2,158, COSMOS-Web n = 2,472 (total 5,203). Treatment 2 to 3 years.
- **Figures:** Pooled multivitamin vs placebo: episodic memory 0.06 SD (95% CI 0.03 to 0.10, P = 0.0007); global cognition 0.07 SD (95% CI 0.03 to 0.11). Authors state the global effect equals about 2 years of slower cognitive aging. COSMOS-Clinic alone: episodic memory 0.12 (95% CI 0.002 to 0.23); global 0.06 (−0.003 to 0.13, not significant); executive function 0.04 (−0.04 to 0.11, not significant).
- **Limitations:** Clinic memory result is on 573 people with a CI barely above zero; subgroup analyses not adjusted for multiple comparisons; cognitive test scores rather than clinical dementia; population largely non-Hispanic White (shown for COSMOS-Mind).

## 3. Physicians' Health Study II: cancer **(abstract via search)**
- **Citation:** Gaziano JM, Sesso HD, Christen WG, et al. Multivitamins in the prevention of cancer in men: the Physicians' Health Study II randomized controlled trial. *JAMA.* 2012;308(18):1871-1880. (One source gave different volume/page numbers; verify against JAMA.) Summary: https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2013/0315/p444.html ; trial: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00270647
- **Design/population:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled; 14,641 male US physicians aged 50+; 1997 to 2011.
- **Figures:** Total cancer 17.0 events (multivitamin) vs 18.3 (placebo) per 1,000 person-years. Effect concentrated in men with no parental history of cancer (HR 0.86; 95% CI 0.76 to 0.98). The AFP review noted the overall significant result depended on an adjusted analysis and concluded the effect is minimal, if any. Lead author Gaziano called the effect modest.
- **Limitations:** Male physicians only (well nourished, highly educated); modest effect; subgroup finding; funding included industry (BASF, Pfizer, DSM, per Grodstein sub-study).

## 4. Physicians' Health Study II: cardiovascular disease **(abstract via search)**
- **Citation:** Sesso HD, Christen WG, Bubes V, et al. Multivitamins in the prevention of cardiovascular disease in men. *JAMA.* 2012;308(17):1751-1760.
- **Figures:** 867 major cardiovascular events (multivitamin) vs 856 (placebo), not significant; stroke and cardiovascular death also not significantly different.
- **Limitations:** same population as above.

## 5. Physicians' Health Study II: cataract **(abstract via search / news summaries)**
- **Citation:** Christen WG, Glynn RJ, Manson JE, et al. Effects of multivitamin supplement on cataract and age-related macular degeneration in a randomized trial of male physicians. *Ophthalmology.* 2014;121(2):525-534.
- **Figures:** 14,641 US male physicians aged 50+, 1997 to 2011. Cataract 872 cases (multivitamin) vs 945 (placebo), about 9% lower risk (exact hazard ratio not seen; the script uses the counts and "about 9 percent fewer"). Nuclear cataract 13 to 14% lower (not used). Visually significant AMD slightly higher with multivitamin, 152 vs 129, not significant (not used).
- **Limitations:** Well-nourished men; authors said findings may not extend to women or less-nourished populations. I did not open the primary abstract page; counts come from several consistent news summaries.

## 6. Physicians' Health Study II: cognition **(abstract via search)**
- **Citation:** Grodstein F, O'Brien J, Kang JH, et al. Long-term multivitamin supplementation and cognitive function in men: a randomized trial. *Ann Intern Med.* 2013;159(12):806-814. PMID 24490265. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24490265/
- **Figures:** 5,947 male physicians aged 65+; up to 4 telephone assessments over 12 years. Global composite mean difference −0.01 SU (95% CI −0.04 to 0.02); verbal memory −0.005 SU (95% CI −0.04 to 0.03): no difference.
- **Limitations:** Male, highly educated, possibly too well nourished to show a benefit (authors' suggestion).

## 7. USPSTF 2022 recommendation **(page opened for the final statement; 2014 abstract opened on PubMed)**
- **Citation:** US Preventive Services Task Force. Vitamin, mineral, and multivitamin supplementation to prevent cardiovascular disease and cancer: recommendation statement. *JAMA.* 2022 (final statement dated June 21, 2022). https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/vitamin-supplementation-to-prevent-cvd-and-cancer-preventive-medication ; earlier update: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24566474/
- **Statements:** Multivitamins and single or paired nutrients (other than beta carotene and vitamin E): I statement (insufficient evidence). Beta carotene and vitamin E: grade D, recommends against for CVD/cancer prevention. Not applicable to children, pregnancy, chronic illness or hospitalized people, or people with a known nutritional deficiency. Statement notes at least some trials found at most small harms. Beta carotene linked with increased lung cancer incidence in smokers or asbestos-exposed people (per search summary of the evidence).
- **Limitations:** Covers prevention of CVD and cancer only, not cognition or deficiency prevention.

## 8. Acid-reducing drugs and B12 deficiency **(abstract via search)**
- **Citation:** Lam JR, Schneider JL, Zhao W, Corley DA. *JAMA.* 2013;310(22):2435-2442. PMID 24327038. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24327038/
- **Figures:** Nested case-control, Kaiser Permanente Northern California: 25,956 with new B12 deficiency vs 184,199 controls. ≥2 years' PPI supply: OR 1.65 (95% CI 1.58 to 1.73); H2 blockers: OR 1.25 (1.17 to 1.34). The script says "65 percent higher odds."
- **Limitations:** Observational, residual confounding, diagnosis-coded deficiency.

## 9. Metformin and B12, Diabetes Prevention Program Outcomes Study **(page opened earlier in repo notes: `b12-after-60/arastirma.md`)**
- **Citation:** Aroda VR, Edelstein SL, Goldberg RB, et al. *J Clin Endocrinol Metab.* 2016;101(4):1754-1761. doi:10.1210/jc.2015-3754. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4880159
- **Figures:** Metformin n = 1,073 vs placebo n = 1,082. Low B12 (≤ 203 pg/mL) at about 5 years: 4.3% vs 2.3% (P = .02). At about 13 years: 7.4% vs 5.4% (P = .12, not significant). Mean age 51 at baseline, about 64 to 65 at year 9.
- **Limitations:** post hoc, B12 measured at two time points only. Participants were middle-aged at the 5-year point.

## 10. NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, Vitamin B12 consumer fact sheet **(search-result text; page returned 403)**
- URL: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-Consumer/
- Statements seen: people over 50 should get most B12 from fortified foods or supplements, since absorption from those is usually easier; low stomach acid with age and acid-reducing drugs can reduce absorption from food; adult RDA 2.4 mcg/day; B12 has not been shown to cause harm even at high doses; metformin might reduce B12 absorption.
- **Limitation:** read as search-result text, not the full page.

## 11. VITAL vitamin D trial **(abstract via search)**
- **Citation:** Manson JE, Cook NR, Lee IM, et al. Vitamin D supplements and prevention of cancer and cardiovascular disease. *N Engl J Med.* 2019;380:33-44. https://www.vitalstudy.org/findings.html
- **Figures:** 25,871 US men aged ≥50 and women aged ≥55; vitamin D3 2,000 IU/day vs placebo (2×2 with omega-3); median follow-up 5.3 years. Major cardiovascular events 396 vs 409 (HR 0.97, 95% CI 0.85 to 1.12). Cancer 793 vs 824 diagnoses, no significant difference. Cancer death was reduced in a secondary analysis (about 17%), not used in the script. No excess hypercalcemia.
- **Limitations:** most participants already had fairly high vitamin D levels at baseline; trial was not about bone or deficiency treatment.

## 12. Vitamin D intake amounts **(search snippets only; ODS page not opened)**
- RDA 600 IU through age 70 and 800 IU after (NIH ODS/IOM figures; one trial protocol confirmed 800 IU/day for ages 70+); tolerable upper intake 4,000 IU/day for adults. **Verify against https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-Consumer/ before publishing.**

## 13. Vitamin K and warfarin **(NIH Clinical Center handout via search)**
- NIH Clinical Center patient handout "Important information to know when you are taking: Coumadin and vitamin K" (webharvest.gov copy): sudden increases in vitamin K intake may decrease warfarin's effect; avoid supplements affecting vitamin K unless approved by a provider. The script says only that vitamin K in some multivitamins can affect warfarin and the amount should stay steady, and to tell the pharmacist.

## Honest verdict

**Well supported**
- A standard multivitamin did not reduce major cardiovascular events in 14,641 male physicians (867 vs 856 events).
- The 2022 USPSTF rates the evidence for multivitamins in preventing heart disease and cancer as insufficient (I statement), and recommends against beta carotene and vitamin E (grade D).
- In COSMOS, multivitamin takers had slightly higher cognitive test scores (0.07 SD global in COSMOS-Mind; pooled 0.06 SD episodic memory in 5,203 adults).
- Acid-reducing drugs and metformin are linked with lower B12 status; older adults are advised by NIH to get B12 from fortified foods or supplements.

**Uncertain**
- Whether the COSMOS memory benefit is real, lasting or applies to more diverse groups, or to people who are already well nourished; the PHS II cognition trial in older men found nothing.
- The small cancer and cataract signals in male physicians (modest, partly subgroup- and adjustment-dependent).
- The "two years" translation is an authors' statistical estimate on test scores, not a measured clinical outcome.
- Exact vitamin D RDA/UL figures (not verified on the ODS page).

**False or unsupported**
- That a multivitamin offsets a poor diet or reliably protects the heart or prevents cancer.
- That bigger doses are better: beta carotene raised lung cancer risk in smokers; USPSTF advises against vitamin E and beta carotene supplements for prevention; VITAL's 2,000 IU vitamin D did not reduce cancer or major cardiovascular events.
