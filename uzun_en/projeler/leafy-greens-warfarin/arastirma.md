# Research notes: leafy greens and warfarin

Topic: Do you have to avoid leafy greens on blood thinners?
Angle: On warfarin the safe rule is a *consistent* vitamin K intake, not avoidance. Newer anticoagulants (apixaban, rivaroxaban) have no vitamin K food restriction.

Every source below was opened (abstract or page) unless marked otherwise.

## 1. Cutting vitamin K raised the INR; adding it lowered the INR (randomized crossover + observational)
- **Figures.** In the randomized crossover part, 12 patients on stable warfarin completed two 4-day in-hospital diets, 1 to 2 weeks apart. One diet raised vitamin K intake by 500% and the other cut it by 80% relative to baseline.
  - Vitamin K-depleted diet: INR rose from 2.6 ± 0.5 at baseline to 3.3 ± 0.9 by day 7 (P = 0.005).
  - Vitamin K-enriched diet: INR fell from 3.1 ± 0.8 to 2.8 ± 0.6 by day 4 (P = 0.04).
- **Observational part.** 39 outpatients, 230 anticoagulation-clinic visits. A higher vitamin K intake score had a progressive inverse association with anticoagulation level, and was independently associated with both over- and under-coagulation.
- **Source.** Franco V, Polanczyk CA, Clausell N, Rohde LE. Role of dietary vitamin K intake in chronic oral anticoagulation: prospective evidence from observational and randomized protocols. Am J Med. 2004;116(10):651-656. doi:10.1016/j.amjmed.2003.12.036. PMID 15121490. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15121490/ (abstract opened via PubMed search page)
- **Limitations.** The randomized arm is tiny (n = 12) and short (4 days). The diet changes were extreme (+500%, −80%), not everyday swings. The abstract does not give ages.

## 2. Day-to-day vitamin K intake tracks INR within the same person (observational)
- **Figures.** 53 patients with stably controlled warfarin therapy kept a 4-week daily food diary, with weekly blood tests. Averaged over 4 days, each 100 µg increase in daily dietary vitamin K was associated with a 0.2 reduction in INR. Average daily vitamin K over 28 days was not correlated with daily warfarin dose. CYP2C9 genotype (P = 2%) and age (P < 1%) contributed to differences in dose between people. The authors concluded that consistent vitamin K intake could reduce within-patient variability and improve safety.
- **Source.** Khan T, Wynne H, Wood P, Torrance A, Hankey C, Avery P, Kesteven P, Kamali F. Dietary vitamin K influences intra-individual variability in anticoagulant response to warfarin. Br J Haematol. 2004;124(3):348-354. doi:10.1046/j.1365-2141.2003.04787.x. PMID 14717783. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14717783/
- **Limitations.** Association only. Small sample. Diet diaries are imprecise. The abstract does not give mean age.

## 3. A small steady vitamin K supplement improved control in people with unstable INR (RCT)
- **Figures.** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled; 70 randomized (35 vitamin K, 33 placebo reported in results). Adults on warfarin with unexplained, unstable control. 150 µg oral vitamin K daily or placebo for 6 months, compared with the 6 months before.
  - Change in INR standard deviation: −0.24 ± 0.14 (vitamin K) vs −0.11 ± 0.18 (placebo); P < .001.
  - Rise in percent time in target INR range: 28% ± 20% vs 15% ± 20%; P < .01.
  - Improved control: 33 of 35 vs 24 of 33. Met stable-control criteria: 19 of 35 vs 7 of 33.
- **Source.** Sconce E, Avery P, Wynne H, Kamali F. Vitamin K supplementation can improve stability of anticoagulation for patients with unexplained variability in response to warfarin. Blood. 2007;109(6):2419-2423. doi:10.1182/blood-2006-09-049262. PMID 17110451. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17110451/
- **Limitations.** A supplement, not greens. Only people with unexplained instability. Single center, small. The abstract gives no mean age. Do not tell viewers to start a supplement.

## 4. Review of vitamin K to improve warfarin control (Cochrane)
- **Figures.** Two randomized trials, 100 participants in total, both placebo-controlled: the 70-person trial above (150 µg) and a 30-person trial available only as an abstract (175 µg), which found no significant benefit. No bleeding, clot or death events reported related to vitamin K. Conclusion: insufficient evidence of an overall benefit; larger, higher-quality trials are needed.
- **Source.** Mahtani KR, Heneghan CJ, Nunan D, Roberts NW. Vitamin K for improved anticoagulation control in patients receiving warfarin. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2014;(5):CD009917. doi:10.1002/14651858.CD009917.pub2. PMID 24832594. (Abstract summary read via Europe PMC.)
- **Limitations.** Very small evidence base. Shows how thin the trial evidence is on the steady-intake idea.

## 5. Official drug information: consistent vitamin K on warfarin
- **Warfarin (MedlinePlus, National Library of Medicine, NIH).** "Some foods and beverages that contain vitamin K can affect how warfarin works." "Eat consistent amounts of vitamin K-containing food on a week-to-week basis." "Do not eat large amounts of leafy, green vegetables or certain vegetable oils that contain large amounts of vitamin K." Also: ask your doctor or pharmacist for a list of vitamin K foods. https://medlineplus.gov/druginfo/meds/a682277.html
- **Vitamin K (MedlinePlus).** Adequate Intake for adults: 120 mcg/day (men 19+), 90 mcg/day (women 19+). Food sources include kale, spinach, turnip greens, collards, Swiss chard, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cauliflower, cabbage; smaller amounts in fish, liver, meat, eggs, cereals. Advice: keep daily intake fairly steady, because sudden changes can alter the medicine's effect. https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002407.htm
- **AHA.** The American Heart Association's warfarin patient guide and warfarin card say, per a web search summary, that it is not necessary to avoid broccoli, lettuce, spinach and similar vegetables, but important to keep the amount of vitamin K consistent. **The heart.org pages returned HTTP 403 when I tried to open them, so the script does not quote the AHA by name.** NIH ODS Vitamin K fact sheet (https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminK-HealthProfessional/) also returned 403; its warfarin statement was seen only in a search summary and is not relied on.
- **Limitations.** MedlinePlus pages are brief. They give a weekly-consistency rule, not a precise number of servings.

## 6. How much vitamin K is in greens (USDA FoodData Central, SR Legacy)
- Spinach, cooked, boiled, drained: 494 µg phylloquinone per 100 g (about 3.5 ounces).
- Kale, raw (FDC ID 168421): 390 µg per 100 g (sampled values 369 to 422).
- Compare with the Adequate Intake of 90 to 120 µg per day. Cooking shrinks leaves, so the same cup holds far more cooked spinach than raw.
- Source: USDA FoodData Central API, https://api.nal.usda.gov/fdc/ (queries "spinach cooked boiled", "kale raw").
- Limitations: values vary by variety and season; per 100 g, not per cup.

## 7. Newer anticoagulants: apixaban vs warfarin (ARISTOTLE)
- **Figures.** Randomized, double-blind; n = 18,201 adults with atrial fibrillation and at least one other stroke risk factor; apixaban 5 mg twice daily vs warfarin (target INR 2.0 to 3.0); median follow-up 1.8 years.
  - Stroke or systemic embolism: 1.27% vs 1.60% per year (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.66 to 0.95).
  - Major bleeding: 2.13% vs 3.09% per year (HR 0.69, 0.60 to 0.80).
  - Death from any cause: 3.52% vs 3.94% per year (HR 0.89, 0.80 to 0.99).
  - Hemorrhagic stroke: 0.24% vs 0.47% per year.
- **Source.** Granger CB, Alexander JH, McMurray JJV, et al. Apixaban versus warfarin in patients with atrial fibrillation. N Engl J Med. 2011;365(11):981-992. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1107039. PMID 21870978. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21870978/
- **Limitations.** Funded by drug makers (per a search summary only). It compared drugs, not diets. Median age is not in the abstract, so do not claim "older adults".

## 8. Rivaroxaban vs warfarin (ROCKET AF)
- **Figures.** Randomized, double-blind; n = 14,264 with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation at increased stroke risk; rivaroxaban 20 mg daily vs dose-adjusted warfarin. Intention-to-treat stroke or systemic embolism 2.1% vs 2.4% per year (HR 0.88, 0.74 to 1.03): noninferior, not superior. Major plus nonmajor clinically relevant bleeding 14.9% vs 14.5% per year (HR 1.03, P = 0.44). Fatal bleeding 0.2% vs 0.5% (P = 0.003). Intracranial hemorrhage 0.5% vs 0.7% (P = 0.02).
- **Source.** Patel MR, Mahaffey KW, Garg J, et al. Rivaroxaban versus warfarin in nonvalvular atrial fibrillation. N Engl J Med. 2011;365(10):883-891. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1009638. PMID 21830957. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21830957/
- **Limitations.** Different drugs have different bleeding profiles. Results apply to people with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation.

## 9. No vitamin K diet rule for apixaban or rivaroxaban
- MedlinePlus apixaban page (Eliquis): factor Xa inhibitor; "Unless your doctor tells you otherwise, continue your normal diet." No mention of INR tests. https://medlineplus.gov/druginfo/meds/a613032.html (the diet quote came from a first fetch of this page; the mechanism and INR statements from a second)
- MedlinePlus rivaroxaban page (Xarelto): factor Xa inhibitor; "Unless your doctor tells you otherwise, continue your normal diet." https://medlineplus.gov/druginfo/meds/a611049.html
- The trial design supports this: both drugs were given at fixed doses, while warfarin needed dose adjustment to a target INR.
- Limitations: this is the absence of a diet rule on the drug page, not a food-interaction trial. Other food and drug interactions still exist.

## Honest verdict
**Well supported**
- Warfarin works against vitamin K, so big changes in vitamin K intake move the INR. In the 12-person crossover trial, cutting intake 80% raised the INR (2.6 to 3.3) and a 500% rise lowered it (3.1 to 2.8).
- Official drug information (MedlinePlus) says to eat consistent amounts of vitamin K foods week to week, not to eliminate them.
- Apixaban and rivaroxaban pages carry no vitamin K diet restriction, and both beat or matched warfarin on stroke prevention in large trials.

**Uncertain**
- That a steady diet gives better long-term INR control or fewer clots or bleeds. Direct trials of "consistent greens" diets are scarce. The best randomized data (n = 70) used a supplement in a selected group, and the Cochrane review found insufficient evidence.
- Exactly how much day-to-day variation is safe. The studies tested large swings, not an extra salad.
- Whether the findings apply to all older adults: the abstracts do not give ages.

**False or misleading**
- "You must avoid all leafy greens on warfarin." Official information says to keep intake consistent, and cutting intake sharply pushed INR up in the crossover trial.
- "Greens make newer blood thinners stop working." Apixaban and rivaroxaban have no vitamin K diet rule.
- "Switch to a newer drug to eat what you like." Switching is a medical decision for a doctor; not every patient can use them.
