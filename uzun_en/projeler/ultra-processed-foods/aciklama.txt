Ultra-Processed Foods: What Studies Show

In a 2019 hospital trial at the National Institutes of Health, 20 adults who ate an ultra-processed diet took in about 500 more calories a day than on an unprocessed diet, even though the menus were matched on sugar, fat, fiber and sodium. Large cohort studies also link these foods with heart disease and type 2 diabetes, but they show association, not proof, and the category is very mixed.

In this video we look at what the NOVA label means, what the NIH and UK trials found, what the big cohorts can and cannot tell us, why some packaged foods looked better than others, and five practical ways to read a package at the store.
