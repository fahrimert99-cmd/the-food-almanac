# Research notes: leafy greens, eggs and macular degeneration (AREDS / AREDS2)

Slug: `foods-for-macular-degeneration`. Prepared for The Food Almanac (US audience, mostly 60+).

Tool note: PubMed pages would not load (cookie wall), so abstracts were read through the Europe PMC record for each PMID (same abstract text). NEI and BrightFocus pages were opened directly. Items marked "not opened" were seen only as search-result titles and are NOT used in the script.

---

## 1. Disease background (agency page)

- **Claim:** About 11 million people in the US have age-related macular degeneration (AMD). The National Eye Institute says supplements may help people with intermediate AMD in one or both eyes (to prevent progression to late AMD) and people with late AMD in one eye only (to slow it in the other eye). It recommends healthy food including leafy green vegetables and fish, not smoking, regular physical activity and healthy blood pressure and cholesterol.
- **Source:** National Eye Institute (NIH), "Age-related Macular Degeneration" overview. https://www.nei.nih.gov/learn-about-eye-health/eye-conditions-and-diseases/age-related-macular-degeneration
- **Limitations:** The page gives no risk-reduction percentages and no formula doses. Basic descriptions of the macula and drusen in the script are textbook-level and also appear in the BrightFocus and AREDS abstracts (drusen, dilated exam).

## 2. Diet and risk of AMD: Nurses' Health Study + Health Professionals Follow-up Study

- **Citation:** Wu J, Cho E, Willett WC, Sastry SM, Schaumberg DA. Intakes of Lutein, Zeaxanthin, and Other Carotenoids and Age-Related Macular Degeneration During 2 Decades of Prospective Follow-up. JAMA Ophthalmol. 2015;133(12):1415-1424. doi:10.1001/jamaophthalmol.2015.3590. PMID 26447482. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26447482/
- **Design/population:** Two prospective cohorts. Nurses' Health Study (followed 1984 to May 2010) and Health Professionals Follow-up Study (1986 to January 2010). 63,443 women and 38,603 men (102,046 total), aged 50+, free of AMD, diabetes, cardiovascular disease and cancer at baseline. Repeated food-frequency questionnaires converted to a predicted plasma carotenoid score.
- **Outcomes:** 1,361 incident intermediate AMD and 1,118 incident advanced AMD cases (mostly neovascular), visual acuity 20/30 or worse, confirmed by medical records.
- **Results:** Lutein/zeaxanthin, highest vs lowest fifth (quintile): pooled relative risk of advanced AMD 0.59 (95% CI 0.48-0.73; P trend < .001), about 40% lower. Total carotenoid index RR 0.65 (95% CI 0.53-0.80). Other carotenoids (beta-cryptoxanthin, alpha-carotene, beta-carotene) were linked with 25-35% lower risk. No association with intermediate AMD.
- **Limitations:** Observational: association, not cause. Food-frequency questionnaires; health professionals (not representative of everyone; people who eat more greens may differ in smoking, exercise etc.). Compares extreme fifths. Mostly white US health professionals.

## 3. AREDS (original trial)

- **Citation:** Age-Related Eye Disease Study Research Group. A randomized, placebo-controlled, clinical trial of high-dose supplementation with vitamins C and E, beta carotene, and zinc for age-related macular degeneration and vision loss: AREDS report no. 8. Arch Ophthalmol. 2001;119(10):1417-1436. doi:10.1001/archopht.119.10.1417. PMID 11594942. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11594942/
- **Design:** 11-center, double-masked RCT. 3,640 people aged 55-80. Arms: antioxidants (vitamins C and E, beta carotene), zinc with copper, both, or placebo. Average follow-up 6.3 years.
- **Results:** Antioxidants + zinc combination: odds of advanced AMD OR 0.72 (99% CI 0.52-0.98). Zinc alone or antioxidants alone not significant in the overall group. In a higher-risk subgroup, zinc alone and the combination were significant. Only the combination significantly reduced the odds of at least moderate visual acuity loss. No serious adverse effects significantly associated with any formulation. Conclusion: people over 55 should have dilated eye exams; those at higher risk, without contraindications such as smoking, may consider a supplement like the one tested.
- **Limitations:** High-dose pills, not food. Benefit applied to people with intermediate or advanced AMD in one eye, not the general public. (The abstract's "contraindications such as smoking" refers to the beta-carotene formula.)

## 4. AREDS2 main trial

- **Citation:** Age-Related Eye Disease Study 2 Research Group (Chew EY, Clemons TE, SanGiovanni JP, et al.). Lutein + zeaxanthin and omega-3 fatty acids for age-related macular degeneration: the AREDS2 randomized clinical trial. JAMA. 2013;309(19):2005-2015. doi:10.1001/jama.2013.4997. PMID 23644932. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23644932/
- **Design:** RCT, 4,203 adults aged 50-85 at risk for advanced AMD; lutein + zeaxanthin, DHA + EPA, both, or placebo, on top of the AREDS formulation. Median follow-up 5 years. Secondary randomization: removing beta carotene, lowering zinc.
- **Results:** Progression to advanced AMD did not differ significantly from placebo for any supplement group. Removing beta carotene or lowering zinc had no apparent effect on progression. More lung cancers occurred in the beta-carotene group, mostly in former smokers. Authors: lutein + zeaxanthin could be an appropriate carotenoid substitute.
- **Limitations:** Everyone in the trial already received AREDS-type supplements, so it tests the add-on, not lutein vs nothing.

## 5. AREDS2 secondary analysis (lutein/zeaxanthin vs beta carotene)

- **Citation:** AREDS2 Research Group (Chew EY et al.). Secondary analyses of the effects of lutein/zeaxanthin on age-related macular degeneration progression: AREDS2 report No. 3. JAMA Ophthalmol. 2014;132(2):142-149. doi:10.1001/jamaophthalmol.2013.7376. PMID 24310343. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24310343/
- **Design:** Same 4,203 participants (aged 50-85). Lutein 10 mg / zeaxanthin 2 mg, omega-3 1.0 g, both, or placebo.
- **Results (exploratory):** Lutein/zeaxanthin vs none: HR for late AMD 0.90 (0.82-0.99; P=.04). Lutein/zeaxanthin vs beta carotene: late AMD HR 0.82 (0.69-0.96); neovascular AMD HR 0.78 (0.64-0.94); central geographic atrophy HR 0.94 (0.70-1.26; not significant).
- **Limitations:** Exploratory/secondary analyses, not the pre-specified main result.

## 6. AREDS2 10-year follow-up (lung cancer and AMD)

- **Citation:** Chew EY, Clemons TE, Agrón E, et al.; AREDS2 Research Group. Long-term Outcomes of Adding Lutein/Zeaxanthin and ω-3 Fatty Acids to the AREDS Supplements on Age-Related Macular Degeneration Progression: AREDS2 Report 28. JAMA Ophthalmol. 2022;140(7):692-698. doi:10.1001/jamaophthalmol.2022.1640. PMID 35653117. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35653117/
- **Design:** Epidemiologic follow-up (5 extra years; all received AREDS2-type supplements afterward). 3,882 participants (mean age 72.0; 57.7% women), 6,351 eyes. Lung cancer self-reported; late AMD validated with records.
- **Results:** Lung cancer at 10 years, beta-carotene arm OR 1.82 (95% CI 1.06-3.12; P=.02); lutein/zeaxanthin arm OR 1.15 (0.79-1.66; P=.46). Late AMD: lutein/zeaxanthin vs none HR 0.91 (0.84-0.99); omega-3 vs none HR 1.01 (0.93-1.09; no effect); lutein/zeaxanthin vs beta carotene HR 0.85 (0.73-0.98); low vs high zinc HR 1.04 (not significant). Authors: lutein/zeaxanthin is an appropriate replacement for beta carotene.
- **Limitations:** Lung cancer was self-reported; follow-up phase was not randomized; the abstract does not report mortality.

## 7. Cochrane review of antioxidant vitamins and minerals

- **Citation:** Evans JR, Lawrenson JG. Antioxidant vitamin and mineral supplements for slowing the progression of age-related macular degeneration. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2023;9:CD000254. doi:10.1002/14651858.CD000254.pub5. PMID 37702300. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37702300/
- **Design:** 26 RCTs, 11,952 people aged 65-75 on average, who already had AMD. Searched to 29 Nov 2022.
- **Results:** Multivitamin/antioxidant (mostly AREDS): late AMD OR 0.72 (0.58-0.90; 3 studies, 2,445 participants; moderate certainty). Early AMD: about 4 fewer progressions per 1,000; intermediate AMD: about 78 fewer per 1,000. Lutein with or without zeaxanthin alone (6 studies vs placebo): little or no clear difference, e.g. RR 0.94 for late AMD (0.87-1.01; 1 study, 4,176 participants; low certainty). Zinc alone: OR 0.83 (0.70-0.98; moderate certainty). Adverse effects: mainly gastrointestinal; zinc linked with genitourinary problems in men in AREDS; beta-carotene raised lung cancer risk, mostly in former smokers. Primary prevention was not addressed (only people with AMD were included).
- **Limitations:** Evidence for lutein alone is low-certainty; many included trials were small/short.

## 8. Formula and who should take it

- AREDS2 formula: 500 mg vitamin C, 400 IU vitamin E, 80 mg zinc, 2 mg copper (cupric oxide), 10 mg lutein, 2 mg zeaxanthin; usual dose one softgel twice a day with a meal. Meant for people with enough drusen/intermediate disease seen on a dilated exam; not recommended for people with no or only a few drusen (five-year risk very low). Beta-carotene was dropped because of lung cancer risk in people who smoked.
- **Source:** BrightFocus Foundation, "Vitamins for Age-Related Macular Degeneration: Do You Have the Correct Formula?" https://www.brightfocus.org/resource/vitamins-for-age-related-macular-degeneration-do-you-have-the-correct-formula/ (consistent with the trial abstracts for 10 mg / 2 mg lutein/zeaxanthin).
- **Limitations:** Not a primary NIH page (the NEI supplements page returned 404 when opened); doses match the trial abstract for lutein/zeaxanthin. Zinc dose of 80 mg is the AREDS dose; some sources mention lower-zinc versions, and AREDS2 found no significant difference between low and high zinc for progression.

## 9. Food amounts (lutein + zeaxanthin)

- Cooked, boiled, drained spinach about 20.4 mg lutein + zeaxanthin per cup (180 g); cooked kale about 23.7 mg per cup (130 g). Raw large egg about 0.17 mg (166 µg) in USDA Release 20; another USDA list shows about 0.25 mg (252 µg) per whole raw large egg.
- **Source:** USDA Nutrient Database for Standard Reference, Release 20 carotenoid table (https://corpora.tika.apache.org/base/docs/govdocs1/327/327148.pdf) and USDA NAL lutein/zeaxanthin list (https://www.nal.usda.gov/sites/www.nal.usda.gov/files/lutein_zeaxanthin.pdf).
- **Limitations:** Older database release, values vary by variety and cooking; figures are approximate, so the script says "about" and "less than a third of a milligram" for eggs.

## 10. Eggs: absorption and serum levels

- **Goodrow 2006.** Goodrow EF, Wilson TA, Houde SC, Vishwanathan R, Scollin PA, Handelman G, Nicolosi RJ. Consumption of one egg per day increases serum lutein and zeaxanthin concentrations in older adults without altering serum lipid and lipoprotein cholesterol concentrations. J Nutr. 2006;136(10):2519-2524. doi:10.1093/jn/136.10.2519. PMID 16988120. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16988120/
  - Randomized crossover, 18 weeks; 33 men and women over 60; 5 weeks of 1 egg/day vs egg substitute with washout. Serum lutein +26% (P<0.001), zeaxanthin +38% (P<0.001); total, LDL, HDL cholesterol and triglycerides not significantly affected.
  - Limitations: blood levels only, not eye outcomes; 5 weeks; small. Funding not checked.
- **Tufts/USDA bioavailability study.** Reported by USDA Agricultural Research Service, "Eggs are a good source of lutein" news item, 18 Aug 2004 (Johnson EJ and colleagues, Tufts Jean Mayer USDA HNRC on Aging; J Nutr August 2004). 10 volunteers; four phases (cooked spinach, eggs or one of two lutein supplements, each providing 6 mg lutein daily); serum lutein about three times higher after eggs than after the other sources; researchers suspect yolk components such as lecithin (and fat) help. https://www.ars.usda.gov/IS/pr/2004/040818.htm
  - Limitations: very small; I read the ARS news summary, not the abstract itself, so I do not know whether the eggs were lutein-enriched; the script therefore says only "a small study of 10 volunteers". Funded by USDA ARS and the Egg Nutrition Center (industry). Blood levels only.

PMID 36547942 (dietary nitrate / Mediterranean diet and AMD, AREDS cohorts) and PMID 26541886 (Prog Retin Eye Res review of lutein, zeaxanthin, meso-zeaxanthin) appeared only as titles in search results and were not read, so they are not cited. ## Honest verdict

**Well supported**
- A specific high-dose supplement (the AREDS formula, now AREDS2) probably slows progression to advanced AMD in people who already have intermediate AMD (or advanced AMD in one eye): moderate-certainty Cochrane evidence, OR 0.72, about 8 fewer per 100 with intermediate disease progressing.
- Beta-carotene raised lung cancer risk (OR 1.82 at 10 years in AREDS2), mostly in former smokers; the current formula uses lutein and zeaxanthin instead, which showed no significant lung cancer increase.
- Omega-3 capsules did not slow AMD in AREDS2 (HR 1.01).
- One egg a day raised blood lutein (+26%) and zeaxanthin (+38%) in adults over 60 without significantly changing blood lipids.

**Uncertain**
- Whether eating more lutein-rich food prevents AMD: large cohorts link the highest intake with about 40% lower risk of advanced AMD, but this is observational.
- Whether lutein and zeaxanthin alone (without the vitamins and zinc) help; AREDS2 add-on results were not significant overall and the Cochrane evidence for lutein alone is low-certainty.
- Whether raising blood lutein with eggs translates into protected vision; no trial tested this.
- Exploratory finding that lutein/zeaxanthin beat beta-carotene on late AMD (HR 0.82) is secondary-analysis level.

**False or unsupported**
- "Spinach or eggs prevent macular degeneration" as a proven fact: not shown in a trial.
- "Everyone over 50 should take AREDS2 pills": the formula is for people with intermediate/advanced disease; there's no evidence from these trials that it prevents AMD in healthy eyes.
- "Fish-oil capsules protect the macula": null in AREDS2.
- "More beta-carotene is better for the eyes": the opposite for people who smoke or used to.
