What Garlic Really Does to Your Blood Pressure

In randomized trials, garlic supplements lowered blood pressure in people who already had high readings. A 2019 meta-analysis of 12 trials found about 8 points lower systolic and 5.5 points lower diastolic pressure, though most of the evidence comes from one research group, small trials and supplements rather than kitchen garlic. In this video we look at what the reviews and two key trials found, how crushing and heat change garlic's compounds, how big the effect is next to diet and medication, and the bleeding caution for people on blood thinners.

We also cover what is still unknown, why garlic is an add-on and not a replacement for prescribed treatment, and five practical points for using it safely.
