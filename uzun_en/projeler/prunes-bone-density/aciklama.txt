Prunes After 60: What They Do to Bone Loss

In the Prune Study from Penn State, 235 postmenopausal women were randomly assigned to no prunes, 50 grams of prunes a day (about five or six prunes) or 100 grams a day for 12 months. Hip bone density fell 1.1 percent in the control group and 0.3 percent in the 50 gram group. The 100 gram group did not differ significantly from control, and 41 percent of that group dropped out.

In this video we look at that trial and a follow-up on inflammation, what is and is not known about why prunes might help bones, two clinical trials comparing prunes with psyllium for constipation, and the cautions around sorbitol, sugar, potassium and vitamin K if you take warfarin. We finish with practical ways to use prunes.
