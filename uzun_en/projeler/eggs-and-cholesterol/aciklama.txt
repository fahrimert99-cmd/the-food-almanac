Eggs and Cholesterol: What an Egg a Day Really Does to Your Heart

Do eggs really raise your cholesterol? A little, in trials: more eggs raised LDL cholesterol by about 5.6 milligrams per deciliter on average. But in large Harvard cohorts following more than 215,000 health professionals, up to one egg a day was not linked with more heart disease or stroke, while another pooled analysis found a small link. The evidence is observational and not fully settled.

In this video we look at dietary versus blood cholesterol, why U.S. guidelines dropped the 300 milligram limit, what the meta-analyses and cohort studies show, the 2019 American Heart Association advisory, why some people respond more than others, what we know about people with diabetes, and what eggs are usually eaten with.
