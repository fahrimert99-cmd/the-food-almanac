Vegetables: What Cooking Does to Nutrients

Does boiling or microwaving destroy the nutrients in vegetables? In a laboratory study of 13 frozen vegetables, boiling cut folate by an average of 68 percent and vitamin C by 51 percent, while steaming, microwaving and pressure cooking were named as better at preserving quality. But heat can also make some nutrients easier to absorb, such as lycopene in tomatoes and beta-carotene in carrots, especially with a little fat.

In this video we look at what water-soluble vitamins do in boiling water, what the research found on tomatoes, carrots and spinach, why a popular claim about cooked spinach and eye health is not supported, where the evidence runs out, and five simple kitchen habits. Includes a safety note for people who take warfarin.
