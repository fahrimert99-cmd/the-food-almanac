How Much Protein Do You Need After 60?

The official US recommended allowance for protein is 0.8 grams per kilogram of body weight a day. But a group of geriatric nutrition experts, the PROT-AGE Study Group, suggests that healthy adults over 65 aim for at least 1.0 to 1.2 grams per kilogram. In this video we look at what that means in real grams, what a large cohort of adults in their seventies showed, and what trials in older adults found when protein was combined with resistance exercise.

We also look at why spreading about 25 to 30 grams across breakfast, lunch and dinner may beat one big dinner, where the evidence is thin, who should be careful (people with advanced kidney disease), and five practical steps you can take. Ask your doctor or a registered dietitian about your own number.
