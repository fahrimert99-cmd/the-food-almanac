# Research notes: walnuts, LDL cholesterol, heart and aging brain

Topic: What do walnuts really do to your heart and cholesterol? (WAHA trial, meta-analysis, ALA, portions)

## 1. Meta-analysis: walnuts and blood lipids

- **Source:** Guasch-Ferré M, Li J, Hu FB, Salas-Salvadó J, Tobias DK. Effects of walnut consumption on blood lipids and other cardiovascular disease risk factors: an updated meta-analysis and systematic review of controlled trials. *Am J Clin Nutr*. 2018;108(1):174–187. PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29931130/
- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of 26 controlled trials, 1,059 participants in total, walnut-enriched diets vs control diets. (Seen on the PubMed record; the abstract says "controlled trials". I did not verify randomization of every trial, so the script says "controlled trials".)
- **Results (walnut vs control, weighted mean difference):**
  - LDL cholesterol: −5.51 mg/dL (95% CI −7.72 to −3.29; P < 0.001), a 3.73% greater reduction.
  - Total cholesterol: −6.99 mg/dL (95% CI −9.39 to −4.58), 3.25% greater.
  - Triglycerides: −4.69 mg/dL (95% CI −8.93 to −0.45; P = 0.03), 5.52% greater.
  - Apolipoprotein B: −3.74 mg/dL (95% CI −6.51 to −0.97; P = 0.008).
  - Body weight and blood pressure: no significant difference between walnut and control diets.
  - Authors' conclusion: incorporating walnuts into the diet improved the blood lipid profile.
- **Limitations:** Pooled trials differ in size, length and walnut dose; the average is not a promise for one person. Lipid markers, not heart attacks or strokes, were the outcomes. The abstract does not break the effect down by age.

## 2. WAHA trial: lipids and lipoproteins (2 years, adults 63–79)

- **Source:** Rajaram S, Cofán M, Sala-Vila A, Haddad E, Serra-Mir M, Bitok E, Roth I, Freitas-Simoes TM, Kaur A, Valls-Pedret C, Doménech M, Oda K, Corella D, Sabaté J, Ros E. Effects of walnut consumption for 2 years on lipoprotein subclasses among healthy elders: findings from the WAHA randomized controlled trial. *Circulation*. 2021;144(13):1083–1085. doi:10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.121.054051. PMID 34455809. Full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8478315
- **Design:** Randomized, parallel, two-center (Barcelona, Spain, and Loma Linda, California) 2-year trial. Lipoproteins were a prespecified secondary outcome. Walnut group: about 15% of daily energy as walnuts (30–60 g/day, about 1–2 oz); control group: walnut-free diet, habitual diet otherwise (free-living).
- **Population:** 708 randomized; 636 completed (90%); 628 with complete lipoprotein data analyzed. Eligibility 63–79 years; mean age of analyzed participants 69; 67% women; 32% on statins. Mean baseline LDL-C 117 mg/dL; triglycerides 105 mg/dL.
- **Results (walnut group vs walnut-free group, 2-year change):**
  - Total cholesterol: −8.5 mg/dL (95% CI −11.2 to −5.4), −4.4%.
  - LDL-C: −4.3 mg/dL (95% CI −6.6 to −1.6), −3.6%.
  - IDL cholesterol: −1.3 mg/dL (95% CI −1.5 to −1.0), −16.8%.
  - Total LDL particles: −4.3%. Small LDL particles: −6.1%.
  - Triglycerides and HDL cholesterol: no significant change.
  - Sex difference: LDL-C fell 7.9% in men and 2.6% in women (P-interaction 0.007); authors call it a new finding that needs confirmation.
  - Weight: walnut group +0.06 kg (95% CI −0.32 to 0.44); control group −0.51 kg (95% CI −0.91 to −0.12).
  - Results did not differ by site; statin changes did not differ between arms.
- **Limitations (stated or implied):** modest LDL change in people with near-average lipids; sex difference needs confirmation; free-living diets not controlled; physicians could change medications; funded by the California Walnut Commission (several authors disclosed ties); secondary outcome; no cardiovascular events measured.
- **Note on sources:** Figures were read from the PMC full text (via page summary) and agree with two secondary summaries (Healio, PACE-CME).

## 3. WAHA trial: cognition

- **Source:** Sala-Vila A, Valls-Pedret C, Rajaram S, et al. Effect of a 2-year diet intervention with walnuts on cognitive decline. The Walnuts And Healthy Aging (WAHA) study: a randomized controlled trial. *Am J Clin Nutr*. 2020;111(3):590–600. doi:10.1093/ajcn/nqz328. PMID 31912155. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31912155/ (PubMed page itself would not load for me; the figures below come from the abstract as reproduced by search results from the Loma Linda University repository, QxMD and news coverage, and agree across them).
- **Design:** Two-center randomized trial, 708 healthy adults aged 63–79; walnut diet (about 15% of energy, 30–60 g/day) vs abstaining from walnuts, 2 years. 636 completed; modified intention-to-treat analysis n = 657.
- **Primary outcome (global cognitive composite):** no between-group difference. Change −0.072 (95% CI −0.100 to −0.043) in the walnut group vs −0.086 (95% CI −0.115 to −0.057) in controls; P = 0.491.
- **Post hoc, by site:** In Barcelona the walnut group declined less than controls (unadjusted −0.037 vs −0.097; P = 0.040). Barcelona participants smoked more, had less education and had lower baseline neuropsychological scores than Loma Linda participants.
- **Authors' conclusion:** "encouraging but not conclusive for an effect of walnuts on brain health"; further study needed in disadvantaged populations. Walnuts well tolerated, good compliance.
- **Funding:** California Walnut Commission grant, no input in design, data collection, analysis or writing, per the paper.
- **Limitations:** primary result null; the site finding is post hoc and from a subgroup; cognitive test scores, not dementia diagnoses; 2-year duration. I did not use any MRI-subgroup numbers because I could not read them in a primary source.

## 4. Saturated-for-unsaturated fat swap (mechanism context)

- **Source:** Sacks FM, Lichtenstein AH, Wu JHY, et al. Dietary fats and cardiovascular disease: a Presidential Advisory from the American Heart Association. *Circulation*. 2017;136(3):e1–e23. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28620111/ (abstract and ACC summary read via search).
- **Key points:** Replacing saturated fat with unsaturated fat (especially polyunsaturated) lowers LDL cholesterol and is expected to lower CVD risk. Replacing saturated fat with mostly refined carbohydrates and sugars is not associated with lower CVD rates and did not reduce CVD in trials.
- **Limitation:** I read the abstract and summaries, not the full advisory. It is about fats in general, not walnuts specifically. A role for walnut ALA or fiber is a hypothesis; I have no primary source showing a mechanism, so the script calls it unproven.

## 5. Walnut nutrition and ALA intake

- **ALA adequate intake:** NIH Office of Dietary Supplements omega-3 fact sheet: adequate intake of ALA 1.6 g/day for men and 1.1 g/day for women (adults); the body converts only a small amount of ALA to EPA and DHA; EPA and DHA come mainly from fish and seafood. https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/omega3fattyacids-consumer/ and the Health Professional version (the pages returned an access error to my fetch tool; values were read from the search-result text of the fact sheet, so treat as moderately verified).
- **Walnut composition (USDA FoodData Central, English walnuts, via third-party mirrors, not opened directly):** about 654 kcal, 15.2 g protein, 65.2 g fat, 6.7 g fiber per 100 g; ALA about 9.08 g per 100 g. Per 1 oz (28 g): about 183 kcal, 4.3 g protein, 1.9 g fiber, about 2.5 g ALA. The script rounds to "about 180 calories", "about 4 grams of protein", "about 2 grams of fiber", "about 2.5 grams of ALA". One oz is roughly a quarter cup of halves (household approximation, USDA measure about 100 g per cup of halves, not verified directly; the script says "roughly").
- **Limitation:** Third-party mirrors of USDA data; small rounding differences between sources.

## 6. FDA qualified health claim

- **Source:** FDA, qualified health claim for walnuts and coronary heart disease, March 2004, as quoted in NutraIngredients, "FDA clears walnut health claim," 1 April 2004: https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2004/04/01/FDA-clears-walnut-health-claim/ . Claim wording: "Supportive but not conclusive research shows that eating 1.5 oz of walnuts per day as part of a low saturated fat and low cholesterol diet and not resulting in increased caloric intake may reduce the risk of coronary heart disease." (FDA's own archived page did not load; the claim is described in the same way in several trade reports.) FDA earlier (July 2003) declined to authorize an unqualified health claim.
- **Limitation:** 2004 claim; I could not confirm current FDA status of the wording.

## Honest verdict

**Well supported**
- In pooled controlled trials (26 trials, 1,059 people), walnut-enriched diets lowered LDL cholesterol by about 5.5 mg/dL (about 3.7%), total cholesterol by about 7 mg/dL, and apo B by about 3.7 mg/dL, with no weight or blood pressure penalty.
- In the 2-year WAHA randomized trial of adults aged 63–79 (n = 628 analyzed), 1–2 oz of walnuts a day lowered LDL by 4.3 mg/dL (3.6%), total cholesterol 4.4%, small LDL particles 6.1%, with no weight gain.

**Uncertain**
- Whether this lowers heart attacks and strokes (trials measured lipids, not events). The FDA wording itself is "supportive but not conclusive".
- Brain benefits: primary WAHA cognitive result was null; a Barcelona subgroup benefit is post hoc. Authors call it "encouraging but not conclusive".
- Larger LDL effect in men than women (needs confirmation).
- How much ALA, fiber or plant compounds contribute versus simply replacing saturated fat.
- Industry funding of WAHA (the funder had no role in design or analysis per authors).

**False or overstated**
- "Walnuts prevent dementia" or "walnuts are brain food because they look like a brain": no trial shows this.
- "Walnuts replace fish for omega-3": the body converts little ALA into EPA and DHA (NIH ODS).
- "Walnuts replace cholesterol medicine": nothing in the evidence supports that; the effect is small.
