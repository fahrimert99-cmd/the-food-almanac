Best By vs. Use By: What Date Labels Mean

Apart from infant formula, no federal regulation requires a date on food in the United States. The USDA recommends the phrase 'best if used by' and describes it as a quality date, yet in a 2025 national survey of 2,069 adults, 43 percent said they always or usually throw food out near its date. ReFED estimates that this confusion wastes about three billion pounds of food a year.

In this video we look at what 'best if used by', 'sell by', 'use by' and 'freeze by' mean, what surveys show about how often they are misread, and where a date is not the whole story: Listeria, a germ that can grow in the refrigerator and that matters more for adults 65 and older. We finish with five practical habits and the honest limits of the evidence.
