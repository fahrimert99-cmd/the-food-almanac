# Research notes: beet juice and blood pressure

Pages opened and read for these notes: PMC full texts and publisher or university abstract pages. Where PubMed or the journal site blocked access, that is stated. Nothing below is cited from a page that was not opened.

## 1. Pooled evidence: meta-analysis of beetroot juice trials

**Bahadoran Z, Mirmiran P, Kabir A, Azizi F, Ghasemi A. The Nitrate-Independent Blood Pressure–Lowering Effect of Beetroot Juice: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. Adv Nutr. 2017;8(6):830–838.** DOI 10.3945/an.117.016717. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5683004/
- Design: systematic review and meta-analysis of 22 trials (2009–2017). 47 intervention groups (n = 650) and 43 control groups (n = 598). Most were short; many were designed to test exercise performance.
- Pooled systolic BP (SBP): −3.55 mm Hg (95% CI −4.55 to −2.54). Diastolic (DBP): −1.32 mm Hg (95% CI −1.97 to −0.68).
- Duration: trials of 14 days or more SBP −5.11 mm Hg; under 14 days −2.67 mm Hg.
- Dose (SBP): 70–140 mL/day −2.37; 250 mL/day −3.22; 500 mL/day −4.78 mm Hg.
- Products with less nitrate (<150 mg per 100 mL) showed larger SBP reductions (−4.63) than those with ≥250 mg (−2.37). The authors read this as a nitrate-independent effect. This is a subgroup comparison across very different trials: hypothesis-generating only.
- Heterogeneity: SBP I² = 29.7% (moderate). Egger's test P = 0.06 (possible publication bias; English-language published trials only).
- Limitations: broad inclusion (mostly not hypertensive patients), varied products, short trials, a correction was published (Adv Nutr 2018;9:274).

## 2. Hypertensive patients, 4 weeks (positive)

**Kapil V, Khambata RS, Robertson A, Caulfield MJ, Ahluwalia A. Dietary nitrate provides sustained blood pressure lowering in hypertensive patients: a randomized, phase 2, double-blind, placebo-controlled study. Hypertension. 2015;65(2):320–327 (online Nov 2014).** DOI 10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.114.04675. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4288952/
- Design: single-centre, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled. 68 patients with hypertension randomized (34 drug-naive, 34 already treated); 64 completed with full data. 250 mL/day nitrate-rich beetroot juice vs nitrate-depleted placebo juice for 4 weeks, with a 2-week run-in and a 2-week washout.
- Results vs placebo: clinic BP −7.7/−2.4 mm Hg (diastolic borderline, p = 0.050); 24-hour ambulatory −7.7/−5.2 mm Hg (p < 0.001); home −8.1/−3.8 mm Hg. No waning over the 4 weeks; BP returned toward baseline after the washout. Heart rate unchanged.
- Vascular: pulse wave velocity down 0.59 m/s vs baseline (0.58 vs placebo); flow-mediated dilatation improved about 1.0 percentage point absolute (~20% relative).
- Safety: well tolerated, no serious adverse events; beeturia (pink urine) and red-tinged stool expected. The authors note that people prone to oxalate kidney stones may need caution with high-oxalate, high-nitrate vegetables.
- Limitations: four weeks (too short for long-term control, organ protection or events), small single centre, optimal dose unknown, batch-to-batch variation in nitrate. Conflict of interest: one author (Ahluwalia) is a director of Heartbeet Ltd, which sells nitrate products (disclosed in the review below).
- Access note: PubMed and the AHA journal page returned errors; figures are from the PMC author manuscript.

## 3. Hypertensive patients, 1 week (null)

**Bondonno CP, Liu AH, Croft KD, Ward NC, Shinde S, Moodley Y, Lundberg JO, Puddey IB, Woodman RJ, Hodgson JM. Absence of an effect of high nitrate intake from beetroot juice on blood pressure in treated hypertensive individuals: a randomized controlled trial. Am J Clin Nutr. 2015;102(2):368–375.** DOI 10.3945/ajcn.114.101188. https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/absence-of-an-effect-of-high-nitrate-intake-from-beetroot-juice-o/
- Design: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover, n = 27 people with treated hypertension; 1 week nitrate-rich vs 1 week nitrate-depleted beetroot juice.
- Plasma nitrate and nitrite rose about 3-fold, salivary nitrite about 7-fold (the nitrate was absorbed). Home and 24-hour ambulatory BP: no difference.
- Limitations: one week, treated patients, small. Actual BP values were not shown on the abstract page I read.

## 4. Single-dose timing (healthy volunteers) and the saliva link

**Webb AJ, Patel N, Loukogeorgakis S, … Hobbs AJ, Ahluwalia A. Acute blood pressure lowering, vasoprotective, and antiplatelet properties of dietary nitrate via bioconversion to nitrite. Hypertension. 2008;51(3):784–790.** DOI 10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.107.103523. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2839282
- Design: crossover vs water, healthy normotensive volunteers; BP study n = 14, 500 mL beetroot juice (~2.79 g/L nitrate). Spitting study n = 6; endothelial study n = 10.
- Peak SBP fall 10.4 ± 3.0 mm Hg at 2.5 h (DBP −8.1 at 3 h). At 24 h SBP was 4.4 mm Hg lower than water, not significant (P = 0.058).
- Volunteers who spat out saliva for 3 h after the juice did not get the plasma nitrite rise nor the SBP fall.
- Limitations: tiny samples, open-label with water control, healthy people only.

## 5. Antibacterial mouthwash

**Kapil V, Haydar SMA, Pearl V, Lundberg JO, Weitzberg E, Ahluwalia A. Physiological role for nitrate-reducing oral bacteria in blood pressure control. Free Radic Biol Med. 2013;55:93–100.** DOI 10.1016/j.freeradbiomed.2012.11.013. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3605573
- Design: non-randomized, no placebo. 19 healthy non-smokers (mean age 23.8): 7 days control, then 7 days of twice-daily 0.2% chlorhexidine mouthwash.
- Oral nitrite production −90%; plasma nitrite −25%. BP rose: clinic SBP +3.5 / DBP +2.2; home +2.9/+2.0; 24-hour ambulatory SBP +2.4, DBP +2.2 mm Hg. BP rise correlated with plasma nitrite fall (r² = 0.56).
- Limitations: no randomization or placebo; young healthy adults; chlorhexidine only; nitric oxide itself not measured. This study did not give beet juice.

**Bondonno CP, Liu AH, Croft KD, Considine MJ, Puddey IB, Woodman RJ, Hodgson JM. Antibacterial mouthwash blunts oral nitrate reduction and increases blood pressure in treated hypertensive men and women. Am J Hypertens. 2015;28(5):572–575.** DOI 10.1093/ajh/hpu192. https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/antibacterial-mouthwash-blunts-oral-nitrate-reduction-and-increas/
- Design: randomized crossover, n = 15 treated hypertensive adults, mean age 65; 3 days antibacterial mouthwash vs water.
- Oral nitrate-to-nitrite reduction and salivary nitrite fell; SBP +2.3 mm Hg (95% CI 0.5–4.0, P = 0.01); DBP unchanged.
- Limitations: tiny, 3 days, mouthwash alone (no beet juice arm described in the abstract I read). Full text not seen.
- Note: I could not verify any trial that gave beet juice together with mouthwash, so "mouthwash blunts the beet effect" is a mechanistic inference supported by Webb's spitting experiment and these two mouthwash-alone studies. The video says so.

## 6. Review (context, product variability, mixed results)

**Gee LC, Ahluwalia A. Dietary nitrate lowers blood pressure: epidemiological, pre-clinical experimental and clinical trial evidence. Curr Hypertens Rep. 2016;18:17.** DOI 10.1007/s11906-015-0623-4. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4729801/
- Narrative review. Notes that nitrate content of vegetables varies with soil, fertilizer and harvest; one product measured 165 mg nitrate per bottle against 300–400 mg claimed. Several trials in pre-hypertensives, treated hypertensives and type 2 diabetes found no effect; reasons (lower baseline BP, dosing time) unresolved. Authors' conflict: Ahluwalia is a director of Heartbeet Ltd.

## 7. Kidney stones

- National Kidney Foundation, "Kidney Stones Diet and Prevention." https://www.kidney.org/node/154344. Lists beets among foods high in oxalate; says people who form calcium oxalate stones may be told by a doctor or dietitian to limit them, plan should be based on their own test results; eat calcium-rich foods with oxalate-containing meals; do not stop calcium foods unless told; drink enough fluid for at least 2 liters of urine a day.
- NIDDK (NIH), "Eating, Diet, & Nutrition for Kidney Stones." https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/urologic-diseases/kidney-stones/eating-diet-nutrition. Limiting high-oxalate foods if you have had calcium oxalate stones; its example list (nuts, peanuts, rhubarb, spinach, wheat bran) does not name beets; recommends a dietitian.
- No trial of beet juice and stone formation was found. The caution rests on oxalate content plus the Kapil 2015 authors' note. (A 1993 paper by Massey et al., PMID 8335871, reportedly lists beets among foods raising urinary oxalate, but I could not open it, so it is not used.)

## Honest verdict

**Well supported**
- Beet juice raises nitrite levels and, in many small trials, lowers systolic pressure slightly: about 3.5 mm Hg pooled across 22 trials, more in longer trials and at higher doses.
- The mouth bacteria step matters: spitting out saliva blocked the effect (n = 6), and antibacterial mouthwash lowered oral nitrite and raised BP by roughly 2–3.5 mm Hg in two small studies.
- The effect after one dose peaks within hours and is largely gone or unclear by 24 hours; in patients it was maintained over 4 weeks of daily drinking.

**Uncertain**
- Size of the effect in people with high blood pressure: about 8 mm Hg in one 4-week trial (64 analysed; author with industry link) versus no effect in a 1-week trial of 27 treated patients.
- Whether the benefit is from nitrate alone (the meta-analysis hints at other compounds; hypothesis only).
- Whether mouthwash blunts a beet juice effect directly (inferred, not tested in the studies I read).
- Long-term effects on heart attacks, strokes or kidney stones: not tested.
- Whether whole beets or salads match juice: trials mostly used juice.

**False or unsupported**
- That beet juice is a cure or a replacement for blood pressure medicine. No trial supports stopping treatment.
- That the effect is dramatic or permanent. Pressure drifted back toward baseline after the juice was stopped.
