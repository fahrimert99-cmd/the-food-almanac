Grapefruit and Medications: Why It Matters After 60

Grapefruit juice can raise the blood level of certain medicines. In a 1991 study of six men with borderline high blood pressure, juice raised felodipine exposure to about 284 percent of the level taken with water. The FDA lists some cholesterol, blood pressure and heart-rhythm medicines among those that can be affected, and a review in the Canadian Medical Association Journal describes older adults as especially exposed.

In this video we cover how grapefruit's natural compounds block an enzyme called CYP3A4 in the small intestine, which medicines the FDA names, why taking your pill hours apart does not fix it, how strong the evidence really is, which citrus fruits are lower risk, and what to ask your pharmacist. Never stop or change a medicine on your own.
