Same lunch. Same portions. Same person. But change the order, and your blood sugar response may change too.

In a small 2015 study, people with type 2 diabetes ate the same meal on two different days. On one day, bread and juice came first. On the other, chicken and vegetables came first, with the carbohydrates fifteen minutes later.

The result was striking: when vegetables and protein came first, blood glucose was about thirty-seven percent lower one hour after the meal.

Why might that happen? Fiber and protein can slow the arrival of glucose. The meal may also slow gastric emptying, and change gut hormones such as GLP-1.

But this is not a magic trick, and it is not a substitute for medical care. The studies were small, and results can differ from person to person.
